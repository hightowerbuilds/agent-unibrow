Why bass necks move

By Mira Castellanos · March 14, 2026

Every bass player has picked up an instrument after a few weeks and found the action higher, the strings buzzing, or the intonation drifting. The neck has moved, and it nearly always comes down to a few causes that work together.

The three causes

The first cause is humidity. Wood takes on and gives off moisture, so a neck swells in a damp summer and shrinks in a dry winter, which changes the relief along the fretboard.

The second cause is string tension. Changing to a heavier gauge, or tuning down for a drop-D set, changes how hard the strings pull against the truss rod.

The third cause is temperature, especially fast changes, like carrying a cold case into a warm room, which can also crack a finish.

What to do

Keep the room between 45 and 55 percent humidity, let a cold case warm up before opening it, and check relief whenever the seasons turn. A quarter turn of the truss rod at a time is plenty.
